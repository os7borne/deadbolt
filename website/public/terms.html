The ground rules.

Effective 23 November 2026 · Covers the Deadbolt apps for iPhone and Mac, the Deadbolt extension for Chrome, the sync relay and this website (together, “Deadbolt”).

In short: Deadbolt is a personal password manager made by one person. Use it for your own accounts. Because only you hold the keys, if you lose your master password and your Recovery Kit, nobody can recover your vault, including me.

1. Using Deadbolt

You may use Deadbolt for your own personal passwords, passkeys, cards and notes, on devices you own or control. You must follow the law and the rules of the sites you store logins for. Don’t use Deadbolt to attack, disrupt or overload the sync relay or other people’s accounts, to probe it for weaknesses without permission, or to store content you have no right to keep. I may limit or block access to the relay if it is abused.

2. Your vault and your keys

  • Your data is encrypted on your device. I don’t hold your master password, your recovery key or a readable copy of your vault, so I can’t reset or recover them.
  • You are responsible for your master password, your 24-word Recovery Kit, your devices and your backup files. Keep a backup and keep the Recovery Kit somewhere safe.
  • If a device is lost or stolen, remove it from another device and change important passwords, as the app explains.

3. Early software

Deadbolt is new, and parts of it are still being tested. Don’t rely on it as your only copy of something you can’t afford to lose. Keep your Recovery Kit and a backup file, and consider running it alongside your existing password manager until you trust it.
