Your passwords, behind your own lock.

Deadbolt is a private password, passkey and card manager that works on your iPhone, your Mac and Chrome. No account to create, no copy on anyone’s server — just your devices, encrypted, keeping each other in sync.

A personal project in active development · not available for download

What it does

Fills, saves and signs you in — without asking you to trust a cloud.

Autofill everywhere

Logins appear above the keyboard in Safari and apps on iPhone, in the menu bar on Mac, and under the field in Chrome through an extension. Face ID or Touch ID confirms before anything sensitive is filled.

Saves without duplicates

Sign up or change a password and Deadbolt offers to save it. Same site and username? It updates instead — the old password goes to history, so nothing is lost.

Passkeys

Create and use passkeys for sites that support them. They’re stored in your vault and synced to your other devices, never to a third party.

Many accounts per site

Personal, work, a bot — each with its own password and passkey. The one you used last is suggested first, three at a time, with a scroll for the rest.

Strong passwords on demand

Random passwords without look-alike characters, or memorable passphrases like copper-lantern-orbit-maple-7.

Cards, identities and notes

Keep payment cards, addresses and secure notes beside your logins. Fill a checkout in Chrome in one click: a card number or security code always asks for Touch ID first, and says which site is getting it.

Careful with your clipboard

Copied passwords stay on this device only and clear themselves after 60 seconds; card numbers and security codes after 30. Secrets are masked until you ask to see them.

How sync works

Your devices talk to each other. The middle can’t listen.

Each device keeps a full encrypted copy of your vault. When something changes, it’s encrypted and signed on that device, then passed through a small relay that only holds it until your other devices collect it.

No account. No server copy.

    No sign-up, no loginThere is no Deadbolt account to create or to breach. Devices trust each other directly, after you approve them.
  • ✓
    Encrypted before it’s storedYour vault is sealed on the device. The master password never leaves it.
  • ✓
    Sync without a server copyThe relay holds only sealed changes, only until delivery, and never more than 7 days.
  • ✓

  • ✓

  • ✓
    Backups you controlEncrypted with your Recovery Kit and saved only where you choose.
  • i
    What the relay can seeA random mailbox ID, the size and time of encrypted messages, the IP address they came from and, for an iPhone, the push token Apple uses to wake Deadbolt. Never their contents. Wake-up pushes carry nothing.

Security design

Standard, well-reviewed building blocks. Nothing home-made.

Deadbolt uses Apple CryptoKit and libsodium rather than custom cryptography, and every file format is versioned and authenticated. Encryption, Face ID unlock, passkeys, the Recovery Kit, device sync with signed device keys, and device removal with key rotation are built and being tested on the author’s own devices.

Vault encryptionAES-256-GCM, with the record’s purpose and key version bound in so data can’t be moved or replayed
Master passwordArgon2id (memory-hard), parameters stored with the vault so they can be raised over time
Face ID / Touch IDVault key kept in the Keychain, readable only after biometric checks on this device; the phone passcode is not accepted instead
Device identityPer-device P-256 signing key in the Secure Enclave; every sync message is signed and sequenced
PasskeysES256 (P-256) keys, “none” attestation, bound to the real website domain
Recovery24-word Recovery Kit (BIP39 word list with checksum) plus encrypted backup files
Lost deviceRemove it from another device: its sync access is revoked and the vault key is rotated

Questions

Good to know

What if I forget my master password?

Any device you can still open with Face ID or Touch ID can set a new one. If none can, your 24-word Recovery Kit lets you choose a new password. Because nobody else holds your keys, there’s no “reset by email”.

What if my phone is stolen?

Your vault on it stays encrypted, and opening Deadbolt needs your face, fingerprint or master password — not the phone’s passcode. From your Mac, remove the phone: it loses sync access, the vault key is rotated, and it’s told to wipe itself if it comes back online.

What if I lose every device?

Use your Recovery Kit with one of your encrypted backup files. Keep the printed kit somewhere safe and away from your devices.

Which devices and browsers does it work on?

iPhone (iOS 18 and later), Mac (macOS 15 and later), and Chrome or another Chromium browser on the Mac through an extension. The extension doesn’t keep anything itself: it asks the Mac app, which unlocks with Touch ID and locks again.

Can I download it?

Not yet. Deadbolt is a personal project being built and tested on its author’s own devices. This page explains how it works; there’s no download or sign-up.
